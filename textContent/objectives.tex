The Last Breath Reactor project is organized around three interdependent primary engineering objectives:

\textbf{Objective 1: Nonproliferation Through Plutonium Reduction}\\
Reduce total plutonium-239 inventory in spent fuel by $\geq$20\% while increasing the relative fraction $^{240}$Pu.

\textbf{Objective 2: Sustained Criticality with Spent Fuel}\\
Maintain $k_\text{eff} \geq 1.00$ for a critical cycle yielding an accumulated burnup of $\geq$60 GWd/t for an initial enrichment of $4.0$ wt.\%.

\textbf{Objective 3: Thermal Safety at Commercial Scale}\\
Maintain DNBR $\geq 1.3$, fuel centerline temperature $< 2500\,^\circ$C, and cladding temperature $< 1204\,^\circ$C during normal operation. Achieve a Power Peaking Factor (PPF) of $\le$ 2.5.

These objectives are coupled through design trade-offs. The 6\,cm lattice pitch enables the thermal spectrum required for plutonium burning (Objectives~1 and~2) but yields a low coolant mass flux, which sets the thermal-hydraulic design envelope (Objective~3). The Power Inverter devices, described in \S\ref{sec:neutronics}, satisfies Objective~2 while additionally improving axial fissile uniformity at end of life.
